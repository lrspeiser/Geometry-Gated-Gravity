MODEL SUBMITTED :
	- CATS v4.1 : SL mass model from Jauzac et al. (2016, MNRAS, 457, 2029)


CATSv4.1 mass model includes :
	- 5 cluster-scale potentials
	- 212 galaxy-scale halos
	- 145 multiple images - please note that the multiply-lensed spiral galaxy (system #1) is decomposed in several family of multiple images, corresponding to the different star formation knots identified (23 systems)

The main difference between the v4 and v4.1 models resides in the family of multiple images (more for v4) and an adjustment of the cluster member catalogue.
	- RMS for v4.1 : 0.63 arcsec

The cluster galaxies catalogue was built following the methodology described in Richard et al. (2014) which combines a coulour-colour selection (f435w-606w vs 606w-814w), and two colour-magnitude selections (606w-814w vs 814w; 435w-606w vs 814w), calibrated by spectroscopical confirmed cluster members. We completed our cluster member catalogue with cluster galaxies spectroscopically confirmed by recent MUSE observations (Jauzac et al. 2016).


PRODUCTS :

	- *_gamma.fits : shear map

METHODOLOGY :

The model has been produced for the concordance cosmological model with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, ΩΛ = 0.7 and ΩM = 0.3.

Our collaboration uses LENSTOOL, an algorithm developed collectively by us over the years.  LENSTOOL is a hybrid code that can combine observational strong- and weak-lensing data to constrain the cluster mass model. Here we only use strong-lensing constraints.

The total mass distribution of clusters is assumed to consist of several smooth, large-scale potentials that are modeled in a parametric form for strong-lensing only mass models, along with contributions from many (typically N > 50) individual cluster galaxies that are modeled using physically motivated parametric forms. 

For lensing clusters a multi-scale approach is optimal, in as much as the constraints resulting from this inversion exercise are derived from a range of scales. Further details of the methodology are outlined in Jullo & Kneib (2009) and have been extended to the weak-lensing regime (Jauzac et al. 2012). At present, the prevailing modeling approach is to assign a small-scale dark-matter clump to each major cluster galaxy and a large-scale dark-matter clump to prominent concentrations of cluster galaxies (Natarajan & Kneib 1997) — both modeled using PIEMD potentials. 

In its current implementation in LENSTOOL, the optimization of the combined parametric and non-parametric model is computationally time intensive. Both the multi-scale and the parametric models are adjusted in a Bayesian way, i.e., we probe their posterior probability density with a MCMC sampler (Jullo et al. 2007). This process allows us to easily and reliably estimate the errors on derived quantities such as the amplification maps and the mass maps. The described procedure automatically produces amplification maps as well as robust accompanying error maps.
